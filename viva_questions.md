# Viva Voce Examination Questions & Answers
## Project: IoT Based LED Chaser (Running Lights)

---

### Q1. Why use an electromechanical relay module to drive LEDs when Arduino GPIO pins can drive them directly?
**Answer:**
While an ATmega328P GPIO pin can source up to 20–40 mA (enough for a single low-power LED), this project uses a relay module to demonstrate **industrial load interfacing and power isolation**. Relays allow low-voltage microcontrollers (5V logic) to safely switch high-power loads—such as 12V automotive strips, 120V/230V AC incandescent lamps, or industrial strobe beacons up to 10A—without risking microcontroller burn-out. In this lab setup, LEDs simulate those high-power industrial loads.

---

### Q2. What is an optocoupler (e.g., PC817), and why is optical isolation critical in relay modules?
**Answer:**
An optocoupler consists of an infrared emitting diode (IRED) optically coupled to a phototransistor inside a single IC package. It provides **galvanic isolation** (typically 2.5 kV to 5 kV) between the sensitive Arduino digital logic and the relay electromagnetic coils. This prevents high-voltage inductive transients, ground noise loops, and voltage spikes generated by coil collapse from coupling back into the ATmega328P and causing system resets or permanent chip damage.

### Q3. What does "Active-LOW" trigger logic mean, and why is it standard in relay boards?
**Answer:**
In Active-LOW logic, setting an Arduino pin to `LOW` (0V) completes the circuit through the internal optocoupler LED to +5V (VCC), energizing the relay coil. Setting the pin to `HIGH` (5V) creates zero potential difference, turning the optocoupler off. 
Active-LOW is favored because:
1. Microcontroller pins and bipolar driver transistors typically sink more current to Ground with lower saturation voltage than they can source from VCC.
2. In the event of a floating pin or microcontroller reset, internal/external pull-ups keep the relay safely de-energized (`HIGH`).

### Q4. How was the 330 Ω resistor value calculated for the green 5mm LEDs?
**Answer:**
Using Ohm’s Law:
$$R = \frac{V_{CC} - V_F}{I_F}$$
Where:
- $V_{CC} = 5.0\text{ V}$ (Relay switched rail voltage)
- $V_F \approx 2.0\text{ V}$ to $2.2\text{ V}$ (Typical forward voltage drop of a standard 5mm green GaP/InGaN LED)
- $I_F \approx 8.5\text{ mA}$ to $10\text{ mA}$ (Desired continuous forward operating current)

$$R = \frac{5.0 - 2.2}{0.0085} \approx 329.4\ \Omega \implies \text{Standard E24 value: } 330\ \Omega$$
Power dissipation: $P = I^2 \times R = (0.0085)^2 \times 330 \approx 0.024\text{ W}$, well within the $0.25\text{ W}$ (1/4W) rating.

### Q5. Why is non-blocking `millis()` mandatory instead of the built-in `delay()` function?
**Answer:**
The `delay(ms)` function is a busy-wait loop that freezes CPU execution, halting all background tasks. If `delay(200)` is used, the microcontroller cannot read incoming UART serial characters, process user dashboard commands (`START`, `STOP`, `SPEED`, `MODE`), or toggle the buzzer during that interval. 
`millis()` utilizes Hardware Timer0 to keep track of elapsed milliseconds without halting CPU execution, allowing concurrent serial communication, immediate command execution, and multi-mode sequencing.

### Q6. What justifies calling this an "IoT" project if the Arduino Uno R3 has no Wi-Fi onboard?
**Answer:**
An IoT system consists of three foundational layers:
1. **Perception/Actuation Layer:** Arduino Uno R3 + 8-Channel Relay Module + LEDs.
2. **Gateway/Transport Layer:** Bidirectional UART serial communication bridged via the modern browser **Web Serial API** to a simulated MQTT broker (`college/iot/ledchaser/telemetry` and `control`).
3. **Application/Cloud Layer:** A web dashboard providing remote telemetry, mode dispatch, and state visualization.
While the Uno lacks an onboard RF radio, the host acting as an edge gateway satisfies the standard IoT gateway architecture. The project also documents the native upgrade to an ESP32/ESP8266 SoC.

### Q7. What is a flyback (freewheeling) diode, and where is it located on the relay module?
**Answer:**
A flyback diode (typically 1N4148 or SMD equivalent) is connected in reverse bias across each relay coil. An inductor (relay coil) stores energy in a magnetic field ($E = \frac{1}{2} L I^2$). When the driver transistor turns off, the collapsing magnetic field generates a reverse high-voltage transient ($V = -L \frac{di}{dt}$), which can exceed several hundred volts. The flyback diode clamps this transient safely across the coil, protecting the switching transistor from avalanche breakdown.

### Q8. What is the fundamental mechanical limitation of electromagnetic relays in LED chasing?
**Answer:**
Electromechanical relays rely on physical armatures and spring-loaded contacts. Their key limitations are:
1. **Switching Speed:** Mechanical operate time is ~10 ms and release time is ~5 ms. Chasing speeds faster than 30–50 ms cause incomplete contact closure or severe contact bounce.
2. **Mechanical Lifespan:** Rated for $\sim 10^5$ (electrical) to $10^7$ (mechanical) cycles. At a fast 100 ms chase rate, a relay switches 10 times a second, consuming its rated lifespan in days.
*Solution for high-speed chasing:* Solid State Relays (SSR) or MOSFET switches.

### Q9. What are COM, NO, and NC terminals on a SPDT relay?
**Answer:**
- **COM (Common):** The movable pole terminal connected to the switching arm.
- **NO (Normally Open):** A terminal that is electrically disconnected from COM when the relay coil is de-energized. It connects to COM only when the coil is energized (our circuit uses NO).
- **NC (Normally Closed):** A terminal that is continuously connected to COM when the relay coil is de-energized, and breaks contact when energized.

### Q10. Why is an external 9V 1A SMPS adapter recommended via the DC barrel jack?
**Answer:**
Each 5V Songle relay coil consumes approximately 70–80 mA when energized. If all 8 relays are energized simultaneously (e.g., in Mode 4: All-Blink), the total coil draw is $8 \times 75\text{ mA} = 600\text{ mA}$, plus the Arduino ATmega328P and LEDs ($\sim 100\text{ mA}$), totaling $\sim 700\text{ mA}$.
A standard computer USB 2.0 port is current-limited to 500 mA, which causes voltage dips, USB port cutoffs, or microcontroller brownout resets. A dedicated 9V 1A adapter supplies regulated current through the onboard NCP1117 5V linear regulator.

### Q11. How does the Web Serial API function in modern web browsers?
**Answer:**
The Web Serial API provides a direct, secure bridge between web applications and serial peripheral devices (RS-232, CDC-ACM USB virtual COM ports). By calling `navigator.serial.requestPort()` and `port.open({ baudRate: 115200 })`, JavaScript can read from and write to microcontrollers via `ReadableStream` and `WritableStream` streams without requiring third-party browser plugins, local Node.js servers, or native client software.

### Q12. What role would MQTT play when upgrading this system to an ESP32 or ESP8266?
**Answer:**
MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport) is an ultra-lightweight, publish/subscribe messaging protocol designed for constrained IoT devices and low-bandwidth networks. 
- The ESP32 connects to Wi-Fi and subscribes to `ledchaser/control` (receiving commands: `START`, `MODE`, `SPEED`).
- The ESP32 periodically publishes hardware status and telemetry to `ledchaser/telemetry`.
- Any MQTT broker (e.g., HiveMQ, EMQX, Mosquitto) distributes messages globally to web dashboards, smartphone apps, or cloud storage with QoS guarantees.

### Q13. What is relay contact bounce (chatter), and does it create issues in this circuit?
**Answer:**
Contact bounce occurs when the mechanical contacts physically collide upon closing, bouncing elastically for 1 to 5 ms before settling into firm electrical contact. 
In high-frequency digital logic inputs (e.g., microcontroller buttons), bounce causes multiple false triggers. However, for driving simple LEDs or incandescent lamps, contact bounce of a few milliseconds is imperceptible to the human eye and does not harm the lighting load.

### Q14. What is the difference between an Active Buzzer and a Passive Buzzer?
**Answer:**
- **Active Buzzer (used here):** Contains an internal oscillating circuit. Applying a steady DC voltage (+5V on D10) immediately triggers an acoustic tone at a fixed resonant frequency (typically ~2.3–2.5 kHz).
- **Passive Buzzer:** Contains only a piezo transducer or magnetic coil without an internal oscillator. It requires an external AC square wave signal (such as Arduino's `tone(pin, freq)` PWM output) to generate sound.

### Q15. How would you modify the circuit to safely control 230V AC mains incandescent running lights?
**Answer:**
1. **Terminal Rewiring:** Disconnect the +5V rail from the relay COM terminals. Connect AC Mains Live (Phase) to the common COM bus. Connect each relay NO terminal to one terminal of each 230V AC bulb holder. Connect all bulb holder return terminals to AC Mains Neutral.
2. **Safety Measures:**
   - Enclose all high-voltage wiring in a non-conductive, grounded plastic or metal enclosure.
   - Install a 5A fast-blow inline fuse on the AC Live line.
   - Maintain physical creepage and clearance distances ($\ge 6\text{ mm}$) between AC tracks and low-voltage DC logic.
   - Ensure earth ground is bonded to any exposed metal chassis.
